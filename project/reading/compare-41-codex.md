## Differences that matter for the essay

1. **How narrowly to organize the sura.** Claude reads it as an extended answer to the refusal in 41:5; Codex reads accountability for receiving revelation and signs more broadly, with consolation and training of the messenger as an alternative. Both identify the return of hearing language and both retain 41:25 as a difficulty. Claude's narrow account has to explain how 41:45–51 advances that particular reply; the broader account risks becoming a theme that would survive almost any order.
2. **What order establishes.** Claude says 41:9–12 has to precede the warning in 13 because its condition concerns rejection of the creation argument. Its own evidence table gives the rival possibility that 13 resumes the turning away already reported in 4. The existing sequence supports the first reading but does not establish that stronger necessity claim. My essay uses the more limited effect of showing bodily testimony before reporting the disruption tactic.
3. **How much the messenger claims.** Claude's judgment says 41:6b claims no standing beyond being human. The same speech explicitly claims revelation. Shared humanity does not eliminate that asserted authority. The distinction matters to an essay about who may correct whom; my essay will make it explicit.
4. **Speech boundaries and confidence.** At 41:7 Codex continues the Prophet's speech confidently; Claude leaves it open. At 12b Codex treats divine first-person speech as the latest return to the framing voice; Claude allows a Prophet reciting divine words. That alternative partly concerns the annotation level: actual recitation of God's words and the represented speaker need distinguishing. At 15a–b and 24 Codex retains alternatives where Claude chooses the framing voice; at 31–32 Codex chooses angels where Claude allows God as well. Neither the mere absence of quotation marks nor grammatical person alone resolves all these cases.
5. **The skins' speech.** Both accounts leave alternatives around 21–23. Claude favors an earlier end because 22 says “your skins”; Codex allows the skins to continue the rebuke. Naming oneself in a list in the third person does not make the continuation impossible. The essay's testimony argument depends on what is asserted, not on settling that boundary.
6. **What a speech introduction does.** Several kind differences are `statement` versus `other` for a dependent introduction of quoted speech. Many setting differences concern whether a quoted exchange is coded by its immediate reported act or the encompassing narrated sequence. These differences should not be redescribed as opposed understandings of the event or as evidence that one model missed it.

Neither account resolves the relation between divine assignment of companions and culpable human response. Both also distinguish the resurrection analogy in 41:39 from independent verification. These convergences make useful topics for criticism; agreement is not proof of the interpretations or the claims within the text.
