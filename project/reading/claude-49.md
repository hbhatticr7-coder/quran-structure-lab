## Part 2. The account

### 1. What each passage does

**1 to 5, conduct before God and the messenger.** Believers are told not to "put forward" ahead of God and his messenger (1), and not to raise their voices above the Prophet's or speak to him as loudly as to each other; the stated risk is that their deeds come to nothing without their noticing (2). Those who lower their voices are said to have hearts God has tested for taqwa, and are promised forgiveness and a great reward (3). Then, to the Prophet: those who call him from behind the chambers mostly lack understanding; waiting until he came out would have been better for them (4, 5). The reason given is that God hears and knows (1). The callers are the example.

**6 to 8, reports.** If a fasiq brings news, verify it, or you may harm a people in ignorance and regret it (6). The messenger is among you; if he obeyed you in much, you would suffer; but God has made faith dear to you and made unbelief, fusuq and disobedience hateful (7). This is God's favour (8).

**9 and 10, fighting among believers.** Make peace between two parties who fight. If one oppresses the other, fight it until it returns to God's command, then make peace with justice (9). The reason: believers are brothers (10).

**11 and 12, speech and suspicion.** No mockery, by men or by women, because the mocked may be the better; no defaming, no nicknames (11). Avoid much suspicion, because some of it is sin; do not spy; do not backbite, which is pictured as eating a dead brother's flesh (12).

**13, to all people.** They were created from a male and a female and made peoples and tribes so as to know one another. The noblest with God is the most God-fearing.

**14 to 18, the A'rab.** They say "we believe". The Prophet is told to deny it and to have them say "we have submitted", since faith has not yet entered their hearts; their obedience will still be credited in full (14). Believers are only those who believed, then did not doubt, and strove with wealth and selves (15). "Do you inform God of your religion?" (16). They hold their submission over the Prophet as a favour; rather God favours them by guiding them to faith, if they are truthful (17). God knows the unseen and sees what you do (18).

### 2. How the sequence develops

There are three clear turns and two lesser ones.

- **Verse 4** (lesser). The plural "you" of 1 and 2 gives way to a singular "you", the Prophet, and to an unnamed "they". The rule about voices is followed by a case.
- **Verse 9** (lesser). No new address formula; وَإِن carries on from 6 to 8. The subject moves from the community and its messenger to the community and its own members. The link may be 6: a false report leads to striking "a people" (قَوْمًۢا), and 9 treats fighting that has already begun.
- **Verse 11.** The address formula returns, twice in two verses, and the offences shrink from fighting to words and thoughts.
- **Verse 13.** The address changes from يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ (five times: 1, 2, 6, 11, 12) to يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ (once), and God speaks as "We" for the only time. The verse gives a standard of worth, أَتْقَىٰكُمْ, in the root that has run through 1, 3, 10 and 12.
- **Verse 14.** The first quoted speech, and the first of three قُل. From here on the sura argues with a claim; before it, it instructed.

Repetitions that tie the parts together:

- "Faith in your hearts": said of the believers at 7 and denied of the A'rab at 14.
- "Obey": لَوْ يُطِيعُكُمْ (7, the messenger obeying them, rejected) and وَإِن تُطِيعُوا۟ (14, them obeying, rewarded).
- Faith as God's gift: 8 and 17.
- أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ three times, sorting people: ٱلرَّٰشِدُونَ (7), ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ (11), ٱلصَّٰدِقُونَ (15).
- Verse endings on God's knowledge: 1, 8, 13, 16, 18. Against them stand human states of not knowing: لَا تَشْعُرُونَ (2), لَا يَعْقِلُونَ (4), بِجَهَٰلَةٍ (6).

### 3. Which reading explains this best

**The reading I favour.** The sura asks what the name "believer" requires. Verses 1 to 12 give the requirements as conduct: deference to the messenger, care with reports, peace-making, restraint in speech and suspicion. Verse 13 gives the measure behind them: taqwa, which is in the heart and which God knows. Verses 14 to 18 apply the measure to a group that claims the name. The sura's two definitions, both opening إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ (10, 15), mark the two halves: what believers are to each other, and what makes someone one. The ending on God's knowledge of the unseen explains why the test can be the heart at all.

**The strongest alternative.** The sura is a set of separate rulings, each prompted by an incident (callers outside the rooms, a bad report, a fight, a claim by desert Arabs), joined only by the usual address formula and stock verse endings. In its favour: 4, 6, 9, 14 and 17 each presuppose a situation the text does not describe. The verse endings also fit the rhyme, so their content may carry less weight than I give it. And the repeated roots (taqwa, faith, hearts, knowing) are common across the Quran.

**What resists both.**

- Verse 7. How "if he obeyed you, you would suffer" leads into "but God made faith dear to you" is not clear to me, and neither is the change from "you" to "those". Unsure.
- Verse 9 calls two groups who are fighting each other "believers", while 15 limits the word to those who never doubted and who strove. The word has two strengths in one sura. My reading has to accept that; it does not explain it away.
- Verses 2 and 14 together: believers who are loud may lose their deeds, while people told "you have not believed" lose nothing if they obey. The contrast is real and I cannot tell whether it is intended.
- Verse 13 is addressed to all people inside a sura otherwise addressed to believers and about the A'rab. On the collection reading it is an insertion. On mine it is the hinge. The text does not decide.

### 6. Consequences

1. **Faith is judged by God, not by the community.** If the test is the heart and God alone knows it, the verdict لَمْ تُؤْمِنُوا۟ has to come through قُل, as it does, and believers should nowhere in this sura be told to rule on another person's faith. Scope: this sura. Against it: verse 6 expects them to know who is a فَاسِق. That is a judgment of character, though not of faith.
2. **The "better" of 11 and the "noblest" of 13 use one measure.** عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونُوا۟ خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ should mean better in taqwa. Scope: this sura. Against it: any sign that خَيْرًا in 11 means worldly standing. I see none, and no proof either.
3. **"Believer" has two strengths.** The address in 1 to 12 and the parties of 9 use the wide sense; 15 uses the narrow. Scope: this sura, and a prediction for elsewhere, that the line between أَسْلَمَ and آمَنَ is drawn where a claim is being tested and not everywhere. Against it: finding the two always distinct, or never distinct, in other suras.
4. **The endings fit their passages.** Mercy endings should follow faults (5, 12, 14) and knowledge endings should follow matters of hidden state (1, 8, 13, 16, 18). They do. Scope: this sura. Against it: showing that any ending could be swapped with another at no loss, or that rhyme alone accounts for the choice.
5. **The rulings must stand without their occasions.** If the sura is one argument, each rule should be intelligible from the text alone. Verses 6, 9, 11 and 12 are. Verses 4 and 5 are the least so, and count somewhat against me.

## Part 3. The reader's judgment

This part is my own view, written last.

**What I find persuasive.** The sura holds together better than a list of rulings would. The strongest single piece of evidence is the pair at 7 and 14, where faith "in your hearts" is granted to one group and withheld from another in nearly the same words, together with "obey" pointing in opposite directions in the same two verses. I do not think that is chance. I am less sure of the weight I put on the verse endings, since the rhyme would produce much the same words.

**What I find morally defensible.** Verse 6 (check a report before acting on it, because the cost falls on others), 11 and 12 (no contempt, no spying, no talking behind backs) and 13 (worth is not descent or tribe) are defensible on almost any ethics, and the reasons given are good ones: the mocked may be better than you; you would not eat your brother's flesh. Verse 9 is defensible as a rule for third parties: peace first, force only against the side that persists, then justice with no penalty beyond it. Its weak point is that it does not say who decides which side is the oppressor, and that decision is where such a rule gets abused. Verses 1 to 3 rest wholly on the premise that this man speaks for God. Granted the premise, the deference follows. Without it, the penalty (all one's deeds void, unnoticed) is severe for a matter of tone. I read the severity as a sign that tone is being treated as evidence of the attitude in verse 1, but the text does not say that.

**What can be checked.** That humans share a common origin (13) is consistent with what is known. If "a male and a female" means one first pair, that is a different and stronger claim; the words also allow "a father and a mother each", and I am unsure which is meant. That peoples and tribes exist "so that" they know one another is a claim of purpose and cannot be checked. That God knows hearts cannot be checked. That unverified reports lead to regretted harm can be, and holds.

**What is unresolved.**

- The logic of verse 7 and the shift to "those" at its end.
- Which way بِئْسَ ٱلِٱسْمُ ٱلْفُسُوقُ بَعْدَ ٱلْإِيمَٰنِ points.
- Where the speeches the Prophet is told to make end, in 14 and in 16, and who "you" is in 18.
- Whether the ethics of 11 and 12 are owed only to fellow believers. The address and the word "brother" suggest so. The wording قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ sets no such limit, and 13, which follows at once, speaks to everyone. I cannot settle it from the text.
- Whether 15 shuts the A'rab out or describes where they may arrive. لَمَّا ("not yet") and the promise in 14 favour the second. The "only" of 15 favours the first.
