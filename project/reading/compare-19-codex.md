## Substantive differences

1. **Organizing argument.** Codex emphasizes gifts alongside the failure of supposed guarantees: kinship, succession, possessions and asserted divine parentage do not secure the claimed standing. Claude emphasizes the giver of children who does not himself take a child. Both readings draw on explicit recurrences of inheritance, offspring and servanthood; neither establishes that every passage serves one governing purpose. The first reading particularly needs to retain the gifts' positive value, while the second needs to account for the discussion of human boast and conduct in its own right.
2. **A repeated premise is not necessarily a prerequisite.** Both recognize the creation language in 19:9 and 19:67. Claude's claim that reading the last third alone loses premises it presupposes is too strong: 19:67 states its initial-creation premise directly. The earlier scene can prepare its reception without being logically required for the argument. Codex's weaker wording, a prepared premise, should also be read as an account of reader experience.
3. **Questioning and care.** Both narrate the two questions about having a son, the responses, and Mary's practical comfort. The Codex essay uses these against a model of the exemplary person as always untroubled or unquestioning. The passages do not themselves give a general diagnostic test for sincere questioning; that limit remains in the essay.
4. **Audience and scope.** Claude favors everyone as the audience of 19:71, while Codex leaves that audience open. Both leave the manner of arrival unresolved. Most other addressee differences concern whether an earlier audience is carried forward or no new listener is marked. The explicit universal scope of 19:93–95 can be discussed without settling the immediate audience of 71; my essay does that.
5. **Segmentation and voice.** The additional splits at the mention commands reflect different readings of a speech-introducing command. The creative “be” inside the saying in 19:35 is a clearer candidate for separate embedded speech under the packet, and Codex isolates it. At 7 and 12a, Claude chooses God as speaker while Codex leaves mediated announcements open; both note uncertainty. These issues leave the essay's central observations intact but should remain visible beside the tag counts.
6. **Thematic weight and comparative rarity.** Claude proposes testing whether the Merciful name's density is unusual elsewhere. That could assess rarity; similar frequency elsewhere would not erase the name's role beside mercy language in this sura. No such cross-sura test is needed to finish the essay, and none was run here. Likewise, the shared short reply in the two birth announcements is exact, but calling the entire answer identical word for word overstates the match.

Both readers identify the responsibility problem around the sending of devils in 19:83. Both also distinguish the possibility argument about resurrection from external verification. Claude's remembered scholarly proposals and infancy-story parallels remain unverified disclosures, not evidence adopted by the Codex essay. No commentary or external comparison was opened for this exchange.
