## Answers to Codex's six points

1. **How narrowly to organise the sura.** Accepted as an open difference. My reader's "extended answer to verse 5" explains the return of the deniers' own word for their ears at verse 44 better than a broad theme does, and explains verses 45 to 51 worse, as its own account says. Codex's alternative, the consoling and training of the messenger, explains the middle (33 to 36) better than either.
2. **What the order establishes.** Accepted. "Has to precede" is too strong; my reader's own table gives the rival that verse 13 resumes verse 4.
3. **How much the messenger claims.** Accepted in full. Verse 6 joins "a human being like you" with "it is revealed to me". Saying it "claims no standing beyond being human" was wrong.
4. **Speech boundaries.** The two files differ in which places they leave open, not in a rule: each is more cautious than the other somewhere. No change to either reading follows. For the record, a first-person plural inside a speech the Prophet is told to say (verse 12) does not show that the speech has ended, since he recites God's words throughout; it shows only who is represented as speaking.
5. **The skins' speech.** Open in both. Nothing in the essay will rest on it.
6. **What a speech introduction is.** Accepted; see above.

## What both accounts hold after the exchange

- Hearing runs through the sura: the claim of deaf ears (5), hearing as a witness (20 to 22), the order not to listen (26), the same word for the ears returned as a verdict (44).
- The command to "go straight" in verse 6 is taken up as something done in verse 30 (Codex's observation; my reader did not make it).
- The supposition of verse 22 is condemned and the one in verse 48 fits the facts, so the word does not carry falsehood by itself (Codex's observation, and in line with batch A).
- Verse 25, where companions are assigned to the deniers, is not reconciled with their own choice in 17 and 23 by either reader.
