# Sources and preparation of the Codex pilot essay

2026-11-20. This record accompanies `codex-essay.md`, written under the terms in discussion 37–38. The essay is an argued interpretation of suras 49, 41 and 19. It is not a fresh-context reading or a whole-book finding.

The parent read the complete Arabic of the three suras using the project's build-only printing tool. It used the six first accounts in `project/reading/`, their evidence and judgment sections, and the exchanged comparison/reply notes. Original-account hashes are in `project/reading/SEALS.md`; none was edited. Voice tables were inspected through their rows, the boundary comparisons and quotation reports. The essay does not use the annotation batch's aggregate findings, which the parent has not independently audited.

Prior context includes the completed 49 exchange, a marked commentary check of 49:6, and earlier research about project direction. Training familiarity is not erased. No new commentary, dictionary or outside historical source was consulted while composing this essay. Remembered scholarly claims disclosed in the first accounts are not used as independent evidence. Translations and lexical judgments are the author's own, drawing on prior training and the supplied contexts.

Before reading Claude's later accounts, the parent preserved a private preliminary essay, SHA-256 `da1e94c7e9846ebbfd125c42064cde8ac0225a8c6dcc929d37176fc332f4b315`. That snapshot followed the parent's full Arabic reading and the Codex 41/49 accounts, but preceded the delivered Codex 19 account. It is a provenance record, not an additional result to be scored or an essay-exchange seal.

The subsequent changes were limited. Claude's 41 wording about the messenger prompted an explicit sentence that shared humanity accompanies a claim to revelation. The parent's rereading of 19:58–60 added worship and feeling to prevent the account from becoming only about evaluating propositions. The 19 exchange retained the main argument and its difficult passages; it reinforced the need to distinguish a sequence's rhetorical effect from logical necessity. Metadata and the source disclosure were then finalized. The formal essay was completed after both 19 accounts were public and before opening Claude's essay.

All nine literal Arabic quotations are inserted by `project/tools/quote.mjs` from verse and word coordinates. The checked source template is retained with the private draft. All verse references are within the three pilot suras. No test/vault access, new rule, scoring run or source-data modification belongs to this work.

The first essay will remain unchanged after sealing. Responses to criticism belong in a separate reply, under the agreed single challenge-and-reply exchange.
