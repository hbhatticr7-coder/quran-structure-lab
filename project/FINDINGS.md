# What the project has learned so far

Codex, 2026-11-20. A snapshot requested by the owner, based on the recorded experiments, the two readings of sura 49 and their cross-reviews, and the declared vocabulary inventory. This is not a whole-Quran assessment.

We have useful observations, a few promising hypotheses, and several corrected mistakes. We have not yet demonstrated a historically new discovery about the Quran. A targeted source check confirms that the basic verification point at 49:6 is already in Ibn Kathir's commentary. The other candidates have not had a comprehensive novelty review; agreement between two models cannot establish originality.

## The clearest findings from the reading

| Finding | Evidence and significance | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Source credibility and report truth are distinct in 49:6. | The verse characterizes the reporter and requires checking the news before harmful action. It does not simply declare the news false. Both models now accept this correction. It gives the project a concrete example of the difference between grounds for accepting a claim and the claim's truth. | The verse's explicit condition is a particular kind of reporter. A universal procedure for evaluating every source requires further argument. A marked second pass found the basic verification point in prior commentary; this is not an original discovery. |
| The audience and presentation change at 49:13–14. | Verse 13 addresses humanity; 14 introduces a group's claim to faith and an instructed response. Both independent first accounts identified these turns. They are observable features of the sequence. | Why precisely this arrangement was chosen, whether the turns form a unique structure, and how much a thematic explanation establishes remain unproved. |
| The relation between belonging and faithful conduct is a live question within the sura. | Verse 9 describes fighting groups as believers; 14 rejects a group's profession of faith; 15 gives demanding criteria. The contrast survives both reviews. | Separate lexical senses, a distinction between membership and exemplary fidelity, and differences of emphasis or local application remain alternatives. The text has not been shown to require one explanation. |
| Speaker, audience and scope must be distinguished. | Uncertainty about whether 15 continues instructed speech does not settle whether its criterion is general. A general claim can be made to a particular group. This removed a consequential inference in the first comparison. | The exact speech boundaries remain open in places. Resolving them would still matter to the account of structure, even where the proposition's scope stays the same. |

These are substantive results of careful reading, though some are likely familiar insights. The value demonstrated so far is making the evidence and the steps of inference visible, including where a fluent account went beyond its evidence.

The [marked source check](reading/second-pass-49-6.md) was performed only after the exchange. It links the [Arabic commentary on 49:6](https://quran.com/al-hujurat/6/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir) and records what changed: the novelty assessment, rather than the first reading.

## A measurable vocabulary lead

The inventory covers selected forms of zanna, hasiba and za'ama throughout the build partition. There are 66 core words grouped into 61 annotation entries, plus two separately declared comparison entries. These entries occur in 56 verses of 23 suras; they are not 61 independent events.

One reproducible difference concerns grammatical person:

| Selected verb | First-person forms in the build data |
|---|---:|
| zanna | 8 of 27 verb occurrences |
| first-form hasiba | 0 of 21 |
| za'ama | 0 of 7 |

This is a finding about the selected forms in this partition. It does not establish that a form is impossible, that its absence is unusual, or that the verbs have a particular difference in meaning. Different speakers, subjects, constructions and repeated passages may explain the pattern.

A worthwhile hypothesis is that these words differ in how discourse presents or challenges a belief. The next step is to identify the holder, proposition, scope, truth as presented by the text, and grounds separately. The [inventory](inventory/SUPPOSING.md) and [annotation instructions](inventory/ANNOTATION.md) make that question concrete. The first annotation batch has 18 core entries and one comparison entry. A sealed label file alone is not a result of comparing the models.

## What the earlier experiments established

None of the five earlier rules contributes a confirmed improvement to the current scoreboard. One passed under an older method, but the challenge showed that its success was matched by the grammatical control. The current baseline and the accepted rules both reach 73.7% on 848 choices with known options; including 198 further places whose answers were absent from the defining data, both reach 59.8% on 1,046 places. These are results for this defined census, not percentages of the Quran explained. See the [scoreboard](../SCOREBOARD.md).

That negative result matters. Simple tested rules about repetition, nearby roots and selected lexical distinctions have not established the intended extra explanation. It does not show that wording is arbitrary or that context contains no information. A weak predictor cannot establish either claim.

## What the progress amounts to

We have completed one sura's paired first readings and cross-reviews, assembled an auditable vocabulary inventory, and ruled out some overconfident interpretations of our own results. Much of the recent work has improved the method. That preparation is useful, but it should not be counted as a new finding about the book.

The next substantive deliverable is the comparison of the first annotation batch, including disagreements and their Arabic evidence, alongside the further complete-sura readings. That will let us ask whether the distinctions found in 49 illuminate other passages or were tailored to one familiar example.

At the end of this update, Codex's 19-entry batch-A file is sealed and published; Claude's reveal is awaited. Codex's independent reading of 41 is complete and sealed, awaiting Claude's seal before exchange. Completion and format checks are progress in producing the evidence, not validation of the interpretations.

No result so far establishes a unique whole-book structure, demonstrates that the text exceeds human compositional capacity, or settles its origin. The immediate contribution is a more inspectable account of what the text says and which assumptions an interpretation needs.
